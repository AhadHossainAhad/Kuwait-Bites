What to try

Start with these. Filter by the kind of thing you are hungry for.

Main

Machboos مچبوس

Spiced rice with chicken or fish.

Kuwait's national dish. Look for dried lime and a tomato-onion sauce on the side.

Main

Murabyan مربيان

Rice cooked with shrimp in a tangy, spiced sauce.

A good pick if you want seafood.

Main

Margoog مرقوق

A hearty stew of lamb or chicken, vegetables and thin pieces of dough.

Comfort food, often served in cooler months.

Main

Harees هريس

Slow-cooked wheat and meat, beaten into a smooth porridge.

Most common during Ramadan and festivals.

Breakfast

Balaleet بلاليط

Sweet vermicelli flavoured with cardamom and saffron, topped with a thin omelette.

Sweet and savoury in one plate.

Breakfast

Kebab جباب

Soft, lightly sweet pancakes with saffron and cardamom.

Try them with cheese or date syrup.

Drink

Karak chai كرك

Strong, sweet milk tea.

Cafes everywhere sell it. Ask for less sugar if you prefer.

Drink

Gahwa قهوة

Light Arabic coffee with cardamom, often with saffron.

Served in small cups with dates. Shake the cup gently when you want to stop.

Sweet

Luqaimat لقيمات

Fried dough balls with date syrup.

Best eaten warm and fresh.

A day of eating

A simple order to follow on your first day.

Morning
Balaleet or Kebab with a glass of karak. Many local cafes open early.
Lunch
Machboos is the safe first choice. It is usually shared from a large platter.
Afternoon
Gahwa with dates, or a fresh cup of karak.
Evening
Seafood, grilled meat, or murabyan. Finish with luqaimat.

Good to know

A few things that make eating out easier.

Manners

  • Eat and pass food with your right hand.
  • When you are a guest, expect to be offered more food. Accepting a little is polite.
  • Dishes are often shared, so take from the part of the plate nearest you.

Timing

  • Eating and drinking in public during daylight hours in Ramadan is not allowed. Many restaurants open after sunset.
  • Dinner starts late. Busy restaurants fill up after 8 pm.
  • Opening hours change on public holidays, so check ahead.

Practical

  • Tap water is not usually drunk. Order bottled water.
  • Many places accept cards, but keep a little cash for small cafes.
  • Tell the staff about allergies. Nuts, sesame and dairy are common.

Kuwait Bites is a beginner guide. Dishes and customs vary by family and region, so ask locally when in doubt.
